# Meaningful guest decisions

## Player outcome

People on campus should react to the systems the player manages. Their destinations must be explainable from personal state and visible conditions.

## Guest state

- Hunger and thirst
- Energy and bladder need
- Happiness/mood
- Spending budget
- Queue patience
- Attraction and product preferences

## Decision inputs

- Need relief offered by a destination
- Product affordability and price value
- Available stock and shop opening hours
- Queue length relative to patience
- Campus quality
- Personal preference weights
- Active event ticket price and appeal

## Rules

- Closed, empty, or unaffordable destinations are excluded.
- Longer queues reduce appeal more strongly for impatient guests.
- Higher prices reduce store appeal even when affordable.
- Using a facility relieves its matching need.
- Completed interactions are recorded by day and destination.

## Acceptance examples

1. A very thirsty guest prioritises an available water fountain.
2. A guest with €3.99 cannot choose a €4.00 product.
3. A patient guest rates the same queue more highly than an impatient guest.
4. Shopping preference can make Merch Lab outrank Atlantic Café when both are viable.
